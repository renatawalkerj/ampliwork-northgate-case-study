## What each stakeholder actually cares about — not just whose quote we weighted

Three people whose day-to-day reality determines whether either the VP's or the Head of Tax Risk's goal is actually achieved. Building this out properly, since the diagnosis is stronger built from all three's actual incentives than from picking a side between the VP and the Head of Tax Risk.

**The reviewing analyst** (does the work, see `answers/02-workflow-proposal.md` for the full persona) — cares about not being blindsided by a duplicate-vendor conflict she has no way to see today, and not spending her month chasing suppliers for the same preventable format issues. Success for her: the system catches what she can't see today, at the moment it matters, with evidence she can act on fast — not a black-box score she has to verify by redoing the work herself.

**The hub tax manager** (EMEA regional manager, oversees the analyst) — cares about her team's diagnosis being taken seriously. She named the root cause unprompted ("coding is not the hard part... a third of our supplier master data is wrong") and research independently corroborates her (`research/sap-domain.md`). Success for her: her own complaint coming back as a measured, tracked number (FR11), not reduced to an anecdote — and evidence the fix targets the root problem (data quality), not just a downstream symptom (coding speed).

**The Head of Tax Risk** (signs the returns, holds the veto — the persona that's never gotten this treatment until now) — her own words: *"I care far more about wrong than about slow."* She personally carries audit exposure on every return that goes out — her signature, her professional liability, not an abstraction. What actually matters to her:
- An **audit-defensible trail** for every determination — not just a confidence score, a reasoned rationale she or her team could stand behind if challenged years later.
- **Nothing auto-files without a human check, ever, at any confidence level** — already a hard design constraint (FR3a/FR7), not a concession to her specifically, but exactly what she needs to hear stated plainly.
- **She (or someone on her own team) reviews and approves any new knowledge-base rule** before it can influence future determinations (FR13a) — the review gate isn't generic governance, it's specifically built for her risk ownership.
- A pricing structure that doesn't create an incentive for anyone — vendor or system — to overclaim recoverability, since an aggressive claim under audit becomes *her* problem, not the VP's. This is exactly why the phased-contingency structure (`answers/05-roadmap-pricing.md`) is conditioned on proven human-in-the-loop control before it expands, not offered upfront.

**How this reframes the diagnosis:** the VP's business case ("free people from data entry") and the Head of Tax Risk's actual risk ("wrong matters more than slow") aren't two opinions to average — they're two different, real, reconcilable constituencies, and the analyst's and hub manager's actual day-to-day complaints (dirty data, non-compliant invoices) — not the VP's framing — are what the system is actually built to catch. The proposal reconciles the VP and the Head of Tax Risk **by design**, not by picking one: cash-first sequencing serves the VP's board narrative without asking the Head of Tax Risk to trust an unproven system on ambiguous judgment; the audit trail and review gate serve her without slowing down the analyst's routine batch-confirm work.

**Weight the APAC complaint the same way.** "The invoice does not meet the local format rules, not because anyone coded it wrong" is independently corroborated by real research too (EU Art. 226's exhaustive mandatory-field list and CJEU case law on formal vs. substantive defects, `research/tax-domain.md`) — same elevated evidentiary status as the EMEA claim, not just her account. This means the diagnosis has (at least) two independently-corroborated root causes, not one — the answer to point 1 shouldn't pick a single winner among VP/EMEA/APAC so much as name that dirty master data and non-compliant invoices are both real, evidenced, and distinct from the VP's coding-speed framing. Gap: we've only researched EU invoice-format requirements in depth; APAC-specific (e.g. Singapore) mandatory-field rules aren't yet researched — doesn't block the EMEA-first pilot, but the workflow proposal shouldn't claim to concretely address her complaint until that's filled or explicitly flagged as still open.
