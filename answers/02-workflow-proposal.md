6. **What the model optimizes for, in tax terms — this needs to be plain-English content in the pitch, not just an internal design choice.**

Confusion matrix, defined for this specific determination (does the transaction flag as recoverable):
- **True positive** — flagged recoverable, genuinely is. Captured correctly.
- **False positive** — flagged recoverable, actually isn't. If auto-claimed, this is an incorrect filing: the exact audit-exposure/penalty risk behind the $4M figure.
- **False negative** — not flagged, but actually was recoverable. Contributes to the $8-30M left-on-the-table problem.
- **True negative** — not flagged, genuinely not recoverable. Correctly ignored.

Precision = TP/(TP+FP): of what's flagged, how much is right. Recall = TP/(TP+FN): of what's actually recoverable, how much gets caught. **Accuracy is the wrong headline metric here** — most transactions are probably "correctly taxed, nothing special," so a model that flags almost nothing would score high on accuracy while providing zero value and zero protection.

**The optimization target isn't one number for the whole system:**
- **Auto-processed determinations (no human review before action): optimize for precision.** A false positive here is exactly the Head of Tax Risk's stated fear — "wrong matters more than slow" means tolerating lower recall (missing some auto-processable cases) rather than letting a bad auto-claim through.
- **Routing (what gets sent to a human vs. ignored): optimize for recall.** Never let the system quietly decide "not recoverable" with confidence it doesn't have — when uncertain, route to a human rather than defaulting to no. This protects the $8-30M opportunity without creating audit risk, because nothing gets filed without review.
- **The confidence threshold is the actual dial between these** — raising it increases auto-processing precision and sends more to human review; this is why the threshold tightens over time as accuracy is demonstrated (already in the plan), rather than being fixed on day one.

**Human-in-the-loop, stated for the pitch, not just the architecture:** the system never files anything itself. A false positive only becomes a real filing if the model is wrong *and* the human reviewer misses it — a second, independent check, not a formality. This sentence belongs in the pitch.

**On a current-error baseline to beat:** the brief gives costs of errors ($4M penalties, $8-30M unrecovered), not rates — computing an actual error rate needs a transaction-volume denominator the brief doesn't provide. A rough, heavily-stacked-assumption estimate is possible (see `claims.md`) but too wide to defend as a number. The credible move: **establish the current human-only process's actual precision/recall empirically in Phase 1** (sample past determinations, re-review them) — this becomes both the benchmark the model has to beat and the starting point for the burndown reporting in `answers/05-roadmap-pricing.md`.

7. **A second, distinct capability: invoice format-compliance checking + automated supplier outreach.** The APAC analyst's complaint ("the invoice does not meet the local format rules, not because anyone coded it wrong," `answers/01-diagnosis.md`) points at a failure layer the tax-judgment engine doesn't touch at all — the *document* fails a jurisdiction's mandatory-field checklist (EU Art. 226-type rules; up to 15 required fields) before any recoverability judgment is even relevant. This is a checklist problem, not a judgment problem — lower-risk and more mechanical than the recoverability determination itself. Propose it as a separate, complementary capability: check an invoice against the applicable jurisdiction's mandatory-field list, and where it fails, automate the request back to the supplier for a corrected invoice (draft, send, track, follow up) — directly attacking the specific task she says eats most of her month, not by replacing her judgment but by automating a rote task. Gap: EU field requirements are researched in depth; APAC-specific (e.g. Singapore) mandatory-invoice-field rules are not yet researched — needed before claiming this concretely addresses her situation.
