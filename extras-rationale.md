# Extras — what we're adding beyond the brief, and why

The brief asks for specific things. Everything below is something we chose to add on top of the literal ask. Each one earns its place by strengthening a specific point in the brief — update this table every time a new "extra" gets added, so it stays a real check against scope creep, not decoration.

| Extra | Maps to | Why it adds value |
|---|---|---|
| Evals harness, presented as a roadmap deliverable, not just internal QA | Part 1.4 (prototype must survive a messy input, show the exception path); Part 1.5 (phasing — evals are milestone 1's proof point); Part 2.5 (what has to be true for productization) | The Head of Tax Risk's veto means "does it work" isn't enough — she needs a way to trust it before scaling. Evals make that trust checkable instead of asserted. |
| PIL claims discipline, surfaced as a "how we built this" appendix | "Before you start" (tell us what you used, where it helped, where it led you astray); Part 1's opening ask (state your assumptions on one slide) | Turns "we made reasonable assumptions" into an auditable ledger the panel can actually check — a concrete answer to the Thinking criterion, not a claim about it. |
| Consulting frameworks (Bulletproof Problem Solving, Playing to Win, The McKinsey Way, Flawless Consulting Fieldbook) | Diagnosis (Part 1.1); productization case (Part 2); stakeholder handling; pre-read tone | Structured methods for exactly the judgment calls the brief demands — weighing contradictory stakeholders, choosing a strategic niche — instead of ad hoc opinion. |
| Build Engine as the delivery methodology, framed as roadmap credibility | Part 1.5 (phasing/pricing logic) | The phased, spike-the-risk-first delivery model we're pitching Northgate is the same process used to build the prototype — proof by example, not just a slide claim. |
| Assumed customer profile, incl. a contract-signing date | Part 2.3 (market-sizing arithmetic defensible under pushback); Part 1.5 (phasing needs a real calendar) | The brief omits the numbers a real deal needs and forbids asking the client. Without this, Part 2's arithmetic is unfalsifiable. Tagging every figure ASSUMED with reasoning makes it checkable instead of invented. |
| Commercial-models research (real vendor/specialist pricing norms) | Part 1.5 (pricing logic); Part 2.4 (packaging/pricing) | Avoids sending a guessed number for either pricing ask — grounds both in what this market actually charges today. |
| Information-model appendix (entity-level detail beyond the brief's named list) | Part 1.3 | The brief names the entities but not their attributes or relationships. Gives the panel something real to interrogate if they push on "what breaks if you get this wrong," instead of a thin answer. |
| Q&A/composure rehearsal beyond the scripted curveball | Composure and Thinking evaluation criteria; "we will interrupt... clients do" | The eval criteria say they'll push on the diagnosis and the architecture, not just the one scripted fact-change — rehearsing only the curveball leaves the rest exposed. |
