The Northgate Ledger

What the brief states, what research established, what we assumed where neither applied, the decisions built on top, and the product requirements that follow. Reference for Phase 1 (diagnose, propose, model) and beyond.

1

Case facts

Stated directly in the brief. No analysis added here.

  • Northgate Industries: ~$14B revenue, operations in 30 countries, 38,000 employees.
  • Global tax function: ~120 people. Indirect tax: 24 people, across 3 regional hubs — Chicago, Rotterdam, Singapore.
  • The indirect tax team is responsible for VAT, GST, and sales and use tax "across every jurisdiction Northgate operates in" — all 30 countries, not only the 3 hub countries.
  • Per transaction, three decisions: "whether tax was charged correctly, whether it is recoverable, and where it should be reported."
  • Volume: "every month they work through hundreds of thousands of accounts payable transactions."
  • "Wrong in one direction leaves cash on the table. Wrong in the other creates audit exposure and penalties."
  • Evidence: "supplier invoices in fourteen languages, purchase orders, goods receipts, contracts, customs paperwork and credit notes."
  • Systems: "three SAP instances inherited from acquisitions, a tax engine, a shared mailbox where suppliers send queries, and a set of regional spreadsheets nobody will admit to relying on."
  • Filing: "monthly or quarterly depending on the jurisdiction." Reclaim window: "typically three to four years, after which the money is simply gone."
  • $8-30M/year unrecovered, Northgate's own estimate. "Nobody inside the company agrees on the number, and the width of that range is itself part of the problem."
  • $4M in penalties and interest last year, across two jurisdictions, for incorrect filings. Jurisdictions not named.
  • Two people named as relevant: the VP of Global Indirect Tax (sponsor, holds budget, already presented to the CFO) and the Head of Tax Risk (signs the returns, not enthusiastic, has veto).
  • Stated ask, first call: "Can you build us an AI that reads our invoices and tells us the right tax treatment?"

  • Vendor security review, ~11 weeks, starts at signing.
  • No data leaves Northgate's tenant.
  • Read access to SAP. No writes, any version.
  • 3 SAP instances, not being consolidated.
  • Year-end close is a fixed date.
  • Head of Tax Risk has a veto.

Research findings

Facts (confirmed against a source) and the findings that support an assumption used elsewhere, combined by topic. VERIFIED unless noted.

Tax law

  • EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC Art. 226 sets an exhaustive 15-field invoice requirement across all member states. CJEU case law: the right to deduct shouldn't in principle be denied for purely formal defects if substance is provable — in practice, tax authorities often deny administratively first. — EU VAT Directive; VATupdate; VAT IT
  • This is real in practice, not just case law: VAT IT (working live EMEA cases) names incomplete invoices and missing VAT numbers as recurring rejection causes; a vendor breakdown cites missing/wrong supplier tax number as the single largest cause (28%, unverified methodology); Germany commonly flags a missing Leistungsdatum (date of supply). — VAT IT; Lovat Compliance (ASSUMED); VATupdate
  • Italy, Poland (mandatory 2026), and Spain (2027) are layering national e-invoicing clearance mandates on top of the EU minimum — a correctly-fielded invoice can still be rejected on schema/clearance grounds. — EDICOM
  • Switzerland and Turkey aren't EU members and aren't covered by Art. 226 at all — each runs its own regime. Singapore (10 fields), India (~19 particulars + IRP/IRN clearance above ₹5cr), and China (state-issued fapiao — a fundamentally different model) each have their own invoice-content rules. — IRAS, CBIC direct; secondary sources for China
  • No published benchmark ranks misclassification, dirty supplier master data, and non-compliant invoices against each other as causes of tax-recovery leakage — each is independently documented as real (KPMG, Deloitte, vendor sources), none is ranked.

What this means for requirements and assumptions: jurisdiction needs a parameterized reclaim-window rule (rolling-years vs. return-deadline-bound) and its own invoice-content rule set — one global constant or one hub-level number both fail. Format-compliance checking is confirmed real and belongs in Phase 1/EMEA scope, not deferred. The root-cause diagnosis can't be settled externally — it has to be measured from Northgate's own data. Switzerland/Turkey need their own rule set or explicit v1 exclusion. Italy's floor is the sharpest reason for EMEA-first; India's is why phase 2 targets a jurisdiction, not "all of APAC" — the Cost of Delay logic above, not just build tractability.

SAP & workflow architecture

  • Tax engines (Vertex, ONESOURCE, Avalara) integrate with SAP by intercepting the tax-determination step and writing the result back to the document — incompatible with a strict read-only constraint. — Vertex, ONESOURCE partner documentation
  • SAP vendor master data splits into general (client-wide) and company-code-specific data, including tax classification. Independently onboarded company codes commonly recreate the same real-world supplier as separate vendor records, each with its own tax classification. — SAP Community, Codasol
  • Vic.ai and AppZen both use confidence-based routing: score every transaction, auto-process above a threshold, route the rest to a human with reasons/evidence attached. ONESOURCE's public documentation describes compliance-layer exception workflows only — no public per-transaction confidence mechanism found. — Vic.ai, AppZen, Thomson Reuters documentation

What this means for requirements and assumptions: the workflow must be a side-by-side "extract, determine, recommend" design, never in-transaction write-back. Supplier and vendor-master-record are distinct entities needing an entity-resolution layer. Confidence-based exception routing with evidence attached is a workable, precedented pattern to build on.

Security review & tenant/deployment architecture

In plain terms: "tenant" means Northgate's own private cloud environment — a locked room only Northgate holds the key to. A public AI API sends data into the vendor's own room; the answer is a private instance of the model inside Northgate's own room, reached only through a connection that never touches the public internet, under a contract that the provider will never read the data or train on it. Honest limit: the underlying hardware is still the cloud provider's — no option, hosted or self-hosted, achieves a literal zero-third-party absolute short of air-gapped owned hardware.

  • SIG and CAIQ are the standard vendor security questionnaires; CAIQ specifically probes cloud tenancy, residency, and encryption. Sandbox demos on synthetic data, staged data-access expansion, and shadow-mode operation are documented patterns for building trust ahead of full production access. — Bitsight, SecurityScorecard; vendor/practitioner sources
  • Hyperscaler confirmed as Microsoft/Azure for planning purposes — a settled premise for this exercise, not externally verified. Azure OpenAI Service, provisioned in Northgate's own subscription, private-endpoint-only, no-training-use terms — the primary, presented answer. AWS Bedrock (fully-managed, via PrivateLink) kept as a one-line noted alternative only, not a hedged parallel option. Self-hosting an open-weight model is the most literal answer to "nothing leaves our tenant" but declined — ~15-20% ops overhead, unrealistic for 2 engineers in 8 weeks. — Microsoft Learn; AWS re:Post
  • Who pays: the resource lives in Northgate's own subscription, so Northgate's cloud provider bills Northgate directly for consumption — separate from, and not included in, Ampliwork's fee.
  • Security reviews validating "data doesn't leave the tenant" concretely check architecture/data-flow diagrams, subprocessor disclosure, network configuration evidence, and DPA terms — architectural and contractual isolation are treated as separate questions.

What this means for requirements and assumptions: NFR1-3 (tenant-bound data, private network only, no training use) hold with Azure now confirmed as the answer. The sandbox → shadow-mode → staged-access sequencing is what bridges the 8-week build / 11-week review gap. Cloud infrastructure cost sits on Northgate's own bill, not Ampliwork's fee — which is why the fixed-fee argument doesn't need to price in usage growth.

Market & pricing

  • Ryan LLC sells proprietary tax-recovery software at Northgate's revenue tier (states $4B in client savings). Way2VAT, AppZen, Vic.ai, and MindBridge are named comparable/adjacent tools. ~850-950 public companies rank at $14B+ TTM revenue — no source directly counts companies with an in-house indirect-tax function of Northgate's scale. — ryan.com; vendor sites; CompaniesMarketCap.com, pulled directly
  • VAT IT, Taxback International, and Ryan run contingency as their primary pricing model, not a narrow carve-out, though none publish rates; adjacent AP-recovery-audit firms cite 10-40%, most commonly 20-30%.

What this means for requirements and assumptions: revised — the incentive-conflict concern assumed the AI's output goes unchecked; it doesn't, since the human reviewer is Northgate's own risk-owning team with zero contingency stake. Pricing is phased, not permanently narrow: Phase 1 fixed-fee + contingency on the urgent-recoverables bucket, expanding to contingency on the full ongoing recovered-$ in Phase 2+ once human-in-the-loop control, continuously tracked metrics, and a below-baseline error cost are proven. TAM ≈ 350-550 applies an unsourced 40-55% filter on top of the real revenue-rank count — the weakest link, flag it as such.

Prototype data

  • SROIE and CORD are single-country retail-receipt datasets with no VAT fields; FUNSD's license excludes commercial use. vatnode/eu-vat-rates-data is MIT licensed, with VAT rates and ID formats for 45 European jurisdictions, sourced from the EC's TEDB.

What this means for requirements and assumptions: no open dataset fits as-is — hand-build synthetic invoices anchored to the real vatnode rates. Duplicate-vendor-tax-profile is the recommended messy input, tied directly to the EMEA manager's complaint.

Additional assumptions

Not backed by research or the brief, and not already captured as a row in the Information table (section 4) — see that table for the fuller set. Stated as assumptions because no source addresses them.

  • AP spend for an industrial manufacturer is roughly 40-60% of revenue.
  • "A third of our supplier master data is wrong" — the denominator (records / unique suppliers / $ volume) isn't specified. A third is material under any reading; severity doesn't wait on resolving which one. The specific field that differs across duplicate profiles is most likely tax classification, not confirmed.
  • Automated supplier outreach (drafting/tracking correction requests) is out of scope — a communication system, not a tax-determination system, outside the brief's ask and the build budget.
  • India's IRP/IRN compliance-check extension and China's fapiao model are real, separate later-phase engineering work, not a free generalization of the EU/Singapore design.

Product requirements

Testable, ready to seed a PRD. Grouped by feature, not requirement type — IDs (FR/NFR/DR/EX) stay as-is so citations elsewhere still resolve; only the grouping and order changed.

briefstated directly in the case study, not derived prototypeactually built/demonstrated in the prototype

1. Tax-treatment determination engine

The core capability: read the evidence, cross-reference beyond the invoice where needed, determine the three-part treatment, score confidence, and use the correct (deduplicated) supplier identity.

  • FR1. The system shall determine, per transaction, whether tax was charged correctly, whether it is recoverable, and where it should be reported.briefprototype
  • FR1a. The system shall extract and structure the evidence needed for a given determination from whichever of the six named document types apply — invoice, purchase order, goods receipt, contract, customs paperwork, credit note — not only the invoice.prototype
  • FR1b. Where a determination depends on evidence beyond the invoice, the system shall retrieve and incorporate that evidence rather than determining from invoice content alone.prototype
  • FR2. The system shall compute a confidence score for each determination.prototype
  • FR2a. The determination shall use the entity-resolved supplier tax profile (DR2), flagging conflicting duplicate profiles, rather than silently using whichever vendor record the transaction happens to be coded against — the EMEA manager's exact failure mode.prototype
  • DR2. Supplier and vendor master record shall be distinct entities, connected via an entity-resolution mapping.prototype

2. Confidence-based routing & analyst workflow

How a human stays in the loop at every confidence level, since the system can never execute anything itself.

  • FR3. The system shall route determinations below a configurable confidence threshold to human review, with supporting evidence and reasons attached.prototype
  • FR3a. Determinations at or above the confidence threshold still require an explicit analyst confirm-and-file action — a streamlined batch-confirm step, not autonomous execution. No determination, at any confidence level, is filed without an analyst action (a consequence of FR7, not a design choice).prototype
  • NFR5. The confidence threshold shall be configurable and shall tighten over time as measured accuracy improves.prototype
  • NFR6. Auto-processed determinations shall be optimized for precision; the routing decision shall be optimized for recall.prototype
  • FR9. The review interface shall present transactions grouped for reviewer efficiency, while each transaction remains its own individually addressable record.prototype
  • DR3. The individual transaction shall be the atomic unit of record; no aggregation into a batch record at the data layer.prototype

3. Reclaim-window urgency tracking

Jurisdiction-aware timing, so the queue reflects what's about to expire, not just what's uncertain.

  • FR4. The system shall compute a time-to-expiration value per transaction, from that transaction's own invoice date and its jurisdiction's reclaim-window rule.prototype
  • FR5. The exception queue shall be ordered by urgency (time-to-expiration) in addition to confidence.prototype
  • DR1. Jurisdiction shall be a first-class entity with its own parameters, independent of which hub processes the transaction.prototype

4. Invoice format-compliance checking

A second, separate capability from tax-treatment determination: is the document itself valid before its content is even scored.

  • FR6. The system shall check each invoice against its jurisdiction's mandatory-content requirements as a distinct step feeding the recoverability determination.prototype
  • EX1. Automated supplier outreach is out of scope for this engagement.
  • EX2. Full support for China's fapiao-based invoicing model is out of scope until scheduled as its own phase.

5. Backlog + ongoing processing pipeline

One engine, two input modes.

  • FR8. The system shall process both an accumulated historical backlog and an ongoing incoming stream via the same determination pipeline.

6. Recovery & diagnostic reporting

What gets reported back to the VP and the EMEA manager specifically.

  • FR10. The system shall report, on a recurring cycle, dollar amount recovered to date against the updated remaining-estimate.prototype
  • FR11. The system shall report the measured scale of the supplier-master-data-quality problem as part of Phase 1 diagnostics.prototype

8. Knowledge base & trust-building feedback loop

The 4th named data source (regional spreadsheets) formalized, plus the mechanism that makes "accuracy improves over time" concrete rather than a promise. Every accepted override becomes an auditable, reviewed record — the trust-building answer for the Head of Tax Risk specifically.

  • FR12. The system shall maintain a structured, versioned knowledge base of jurisdiction- and entity-specific exceptions beyond the base rule set, seeded by curating existing regional-spreadsheet content — not by importing it wholesale.prototype
  • FR12a. Each knowledge-base entry shall carry provenance (source, author, date, review status). An entry shall not influence auto-processed determinations until approved.prototype
  • FR13. When an analyst overrides a determination, the override and its stated reason shall be captured as a candidate knowledge-base entry, not discarded.prototype
  • FR13a. Candidate entries shall be surfaced for review (by the hub tax manager) before promotion to an approved, active rule.prototype
  • DR4. Knowledge-base entries shall be a first-class entity, distinct from jurisdiction rules (DR1) and entity-resolution mappings (DR2), linked to the transaction(s) that originated them, for auditability.prototype

7. Platform & security constraints

The tenant/SAP boundary the whole system is built inside of.

  • NFR1. All data processed shall remain within Northgate's own cloud tenant/subscription; no data shall be sent to a public multi-tenant model API.brief
  • NFR2. All communication with any model/AI resource shall occur over a private network path, not the public internet.
  • NFR3. Any third-party model provider shall be contractually prohibited from using Northgate's data for training or accessing it outside the agreed service boundary.
  • NFR4. SAP access shall be read-only; no write access to any SAP instance, in any version.brief
  • FR7. The system shall not write or post any determination into SAP or any filing system; write-back is performed only by an authorized human or downstream system.briefprototype
  • EX3. Consolidation of the 3 SAP instances is out of scope; the solution operates across all 3 as-is.briefprototype

Glossary

Tax & finance
AP
Accounts Payable — money Northgate owes its suppliers for goods/services received. The brief's own "hundreds of thousands of accounts payable transactions" a month.
VAT / GST
Value Added Tax / Goods and Services Tax — a consumption tax charged at each stage of a supply chain; businesses reclaim what they paid on inputs.
Recoverability
Whether tax paid on a purchase can legally be reclaimed or offset.
Reclaim window
The deadline after which an otherwise-valid tax reclaim is permanently lost.
Time-to-expiration

Contingency fee

SAP & technical
Company code
SAP's unit for a legal entity within an instance; vendor tax data is kept per company code.
Vendor master data
The record SAP holds per supplier — general (client-wide) data plus company-code-specific data.
Read-only / write-back
Read-only: viewing SAP data without posting changes. Write-back: posting a result into the live record — always a human action here, never performed by the system itself, at any confidence level.
Exception routing
Sending a low-confidence case to a human for full investigation, vs. a high-confidence case, which still needs a human to confirm and file it — just at speed, not from scratch.
Shadow mode
Running a new system in parallel with the existing process, without letting it act.
Tenant
A customer's own isolated cloud environment — the "locked room" data must stay inside.
Private endpoint
A network connection that never touches the public internet, even between two cloud resources.
