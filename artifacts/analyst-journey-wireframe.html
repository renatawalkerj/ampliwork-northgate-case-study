The analyst's journey

First, why the tech stack itself creates the problem. Then a day in her life, today — and the same day, with the AI system. Each moment reads as: what she does, in which system, and what happens next. Requirement IDs are cited inline, not in a separate list.

The reality — how the tech stack fails her

13 SAP instances (SAP-EU-1/2/3)

Inherited via 3 separate acquisitions — different vendor masters, no shared identity for the same real-world supplier. Vantpoint Fasteners exists as three different-looking vendor records with three different tax classifications, none of them cross-checked against the others.

Confirmed pattern: `research/sap-domain.md` — independently onboarded company codes commonly recreate the same supplier as separate records.

2The tax engine

Determines a tax code at posting — but only as good as the data SAP feeds it. A vague transaction description ("Project Expenses" instead of a specific category) makes a rules-based engine default to a conservative treatment rather than the correct one.

ASSUMED — general rules-based-tax-engine behavior (garbage-in-garbage-out), not confirmed for Northgate's specific engine.

3The shared mailbox

Suppliers emailing about rejected invoices, short-payments from tax disputes, requests for updated tax certificates — the channel where problems surface, never where they get solved.

Brief fact: "a shared mailbox where suppliers send queries."

4The regional spreadsheets

Because nobody fully trusts that the 3 SAP instances and the tax engine agree with each other, she keeps her own shadow tracker of local exceptions and known workarounds — illustratively, something like EMEA_VAT_Exceptions_v7_FINAL.xlsx.

Brief's own words: "a set of regional spreadsheets nobody will admit to relying on."
